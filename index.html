Every summary is generated from real, current articles pulled directly from PubMed — the U.S. National Library of Medicine's database of over 35 million biomedical papers. Each summary links back to its original source so you can verify anything yourself. That said, AI-generated summaries can occasionally misinterpret or oversimplify complex findings, so MedSumAI is best used as a starting point for understanding research, not as a replacement for reading the original paper or talking to a medical professional.
No. MedSumAI summarizes published research for educational purposes only — it does not diagnose conditions, recommend treatments, or replace a conversation with a doctor. If you're making a health decision, always consult a qualified healthcare provider.

After generating a summary, you can ask follow-up questions about it directly — like "what were the key findings?" or "how large was the study?" The AI answers based on the specific summary and sources it just generated for you, so the conversation stays grounded in that research.
Every citation links directly to the original article on PubMed. MedSumAI doesn't invent or paraphrase sources — it pulls real titles, authors, and links from PubMed's public database, so you can always click through and read the full paper yourself.
The free PDF study guides are written to work at multiple levels — high school biology, pre-med coursework, and med school review — covering foundational topics like immunology, cell biology, and genetics. They're a good fit whether you're studying for a class or just curious.
