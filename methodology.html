How MedSumAI works

Every summary starts with real research. Here's what happens between your query and the plain-English explanation you get back.

The pipeline, step by step

1

Query → PubMed search

Your topic is sent to the PubMed E-utilities API — the same database used by researchers worldwide, with over 35 million biomedical citations. MedSumAI searches by relevance and recency, pulling the top results for your query.

2

Abstract retrieval

MedSumAI fetches the structured abstracts of the top articles: title, authors, journal, year, and the abstract text itself. It doesn't have access to full-text PDFs — only what PubMed's public API exposes. Each article's PubMed ID (PMID) is preserved so citations can link directly to the source.

3

AI summarization (GPT-4o)

The retrieved abstracts are passed to OpenAI's GPT-4o with a structured prompt that asks it to: synthesize the findings across papers, write at a pre-med/AP Biology level, produce an evidence snapshot, note key limitations, and include every PubMed citation by PMID. The AI is explicitly instructed not to add information not present in the abstracts.

4

Rendering & citation linking

The summary is rendered with marked.js and sanitized with DOMPurify before display. Every PMID reference in the output is automatically hyperlinked to pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/[PMID], so you can click through to the original abstract or full paper.

Evidence Snapshot

Each summary includes an evidence snapshot that reflects the type and consistency of studies found. These follow standard evidence hierarchy conventions used in clinical medicine:

Rating What it means Typical source types
Strong Multiple high-quality studies with consistent findings Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, large RCTs
Moderate Reasonably consistent evidence, but some gaps Smaller RCTs, prospective cohort studies
Limited Early or sparse evidence; conclusions are preliminary Observational studies, case series, animal models
Mixed / Conflicting Studies exist but disagree on outcomes or direction Various study types with contradictory results

What MedSumAI is — and isn't

✓

Designed for learning

MedSumAI is built to help students, curious readers, and researchers quickly understand what the published literature says about a topic. It makes PubMed abstracts accessible without replacing them.

✗

Not a clinical tool

Summaries are educational. They are not a substitute for clinical judgment, a qualified clinician, or reading the full papers yourself. The AI can make mistakes; always verify findings against the linked PubMed sources.

✗

No full-text access

MedSumAI summarizes only what PubMed's public API returns — typically the structured abstract. It cannot access paywalled full-text articles. For detailed methodology, results tables, or supplementary data, follow the PMID link to PubMed and access the full paper through your institution or PubMed Central.

⚠️

Educational use only. Nothing on MedSumAI is medical advice. If you have questions about your own health, please consult a qualified healthcare professional. AI-generated summaries can contain errors — always check the linked PubMed sources before drawing conclusions.
