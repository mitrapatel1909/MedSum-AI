In plain English

Research in animals and humans suggests intermittent fasting (IF) can produce weight loss and improve some metabolic and cognitive biomarkers, and it alters immune, metabolic, and microbiome-related processes. Randomized trials show short-term benefits for weight and some brain measures, but some trials report tissue-specific harms (hair follicle regeneration) and many reviews note heterogeneity and limited long-term data.

Key findings

  • IF protocols (time-restricted eating, alternate-day fasting, 5:2 and other schedules) are associated with weight loss and reduced fat mass in humans in multiple studies and reviews PMID 37906749 PMID 38955141.
  • A randomized trial in people with obesity and type 2 diabetes found greater percent weight loss over 3 months with IF 16:8 and 14:10 (3 days/week) than with a control diet, and improvements in fasting blood glucose, HbA1c, and lipid profiles PMID 38932663.
  • An 8-week randomized clinical trial in older adults with insulin resistance reported that 5:2 IF produced greater weight loss and had comparable improvements to a healthy living diet in several brain biomarkers (insulin signaling in neuron-derived extracellular vesicles, brain-age-gap on MRI, brain glucose on MRS) and cognitive measures; some cognitive benefits favored IF PMID 38901423.
  • Narrative reviews report that IF alters immunometabolism (immune–metabolic interactions), promotes metabolic flexibility (switching from glucose to fatty acid/ketone use), and modulates the gut microbiome and inflammation—mechanistic pathways that could explain effects on metabolic disease and ageing PMID 38719726 PMID 37828854 PMID 38955141.
  • A randomized trial reported a potential adverse tissue-specific effect: IF inhibited hair follicle regeneration by inducing apoptosis of activated hair follicle stem cells via interorgan signaling, and a clinical trial component indicated reduced human hair growth with IF PMID 39674178.

How strong is the evidence?

The evidence includes recent randomized controlled trials that directly compared IF to control diets and to alternative diets (two RCTs are in the provided set) as well as multiple narrative reviews that synthesize mechanistic and observational findings PMID 38932663 PMID 38901423 PMID 39674178 PMID 37906749 PMID 38719726 PMID 37828854 PMID 38955141. The RCT in diabetes reported results from 99 completers over 3 months PMID 38932663, and the brain RCT enrolled 40 older adults for 8 weeks PMID 38901423. Narrative reviews summarize animal, cellular, and human studies but are not systematic pooled analyses PMID 37906749 PMID 38719726 PMID 37828854 PMID 38955141.

Limitations and caveats

  • Several articles are narrative reviews that summarize diverse studies; they do not provide pooled effect sizes or resolve heterogeneity among trials PMID 37906749 PMID 38719726 PMID 37828854 PMID 38955141.
  • Long-term effects, optimal IF schedule, and safety across populations are not established by the provided abstracts; most human trials cited are short-term (weeks to months) PMID 38932663 PMID 38901423.
  • Mechanistic and animal findings (e.g., effects on stem cells, microbiome, immunometabolism) suggest pathways but do not prove clinical outcomes in humans without further trials PMID 38719726 PMID 39674178 PMID 38955141.
  • One randomized trial identified a possible adverse effect on hair follicle regeneration, indicating IF can have tissue-specific negative effects; this shows benefits may not be universal and requires more study PMID 39674178.
