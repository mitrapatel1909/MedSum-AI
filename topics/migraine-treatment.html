In plain English

Research shows that drugs targeting calcitonin gene‑related peptide (CGRP) — including monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and small‑molecule CGRP receptor antagonists (called gepants) — are effective, migraine‑specific options for preventing both episodic and chronic migraine. Guidelines and position statements now place CGRP‑targeting therapies among frontline preventive choices because of consistent trial evidence for reduced monthly migraine days and favorable tolerability compared with older, non‑specific preventives. Evidence specifically supports some gepants (rimegepant, atogepant) and four anti‑CGRP mAbs (erenumab, galcanezumab, fremanezumab, eptinezumab) for prevention. PMID 38307640 PMID 38466028 PMID 39506371

Key findings

  • Anti‑CGRP monoclonal antibodies (erenumab, galcanezumab, fremanezumab, eptinezumab) have demonstrated efficacy and acceptable safety in clinical trials for prevention of episodic and chronic migraine. PMID 38307640
  • Gepants (small‑molecule CGRP receptor antagonists) such as rimegepant and atogepant are approved options for migraine prevention and have shown preventive efficacy in trials; rimegepant is notable as an agent studied for both acute and preventive use. PMID 38307640 PMID 36739335
  • Systematic guideline reviews (Italian Society for the Study of Headache/International Headache Society and updated Canadian Headache Society guidance) incorporate randomized controlled trial data and endorse CGRP‑targeting agents as important preventive options, noting gaps such as few head‑to‑head comparisons and limited long‑term outcome data. PMID 40277319 PMID 40277321 PMID 39506371
  • Meta‑analytic data in the context of menstrual migraine found that CGRP monoclonal antibodies (erenumab, galcanezumab) significantly reduced headache days compared with comparators, and thus were more effective than triptans for prevention of menstrual migraine in that analysis. (Note: this applies specifically to menstrual migraine trials included in the meta‑analysis.) PMID 39227797
  • Professional societies (American Headache Society) state the body of evidence for CGRP‑targeting therapies’ efficacy, tolerability, and safety is substantial and exceeds evidence for older preventive agents, supporting their use as first‑line options in many patients. PMID 38466028

How strong is the evidence?

The evidence comes largely from randomized controlled trials synthesized into systematic reviews, meta‑analyses, and professional guideline statements summarized in the cited articles. Large numbers of RCTs were reviewed in guideline and meta‑analytic work (e.g., the Canadian update screened many studies and focused on 61 trials for multiple therapies), and the clinical trial program for CGRP agents includes placebo‑controlled RCTs with open‑label extensions cited in position statements and reviews. While these sources report consistent beneficial effects on monthly migraine days and tolerability, the guideline authors note limited head‑to‑head comparisons and a need for longer‑term outcome data. PMID 39506371 PMID 40277319 PMID 38466028

Limitations and caveats

  • These abstracts summarize reviews, guidelines, position statements, and meta‑analyses rather than providing new single‑study data; detailed trial sizes, exact effect sizes, and safety event rates are not reported in the abstracts themselves. PMID 40277321 PMID 39506371
  • There are few direct head‑to‑head trials comparing different CGRP agents or comparing CGRP drugs directly against all older preventives, so relative advantages between specific agents are uncertain. PMID 40277319
  • Long‑term outcomes (durability of benefit, rare adverse effects over many years) and effects in some subgroups remain areas identified as needing more research. PMID 40277319 PMID 38466028
  • Some findings referenced (for example, menstrual migraine comparisons) apply to that specific population and may not generalize to all people with migraine. PMID 39227797
