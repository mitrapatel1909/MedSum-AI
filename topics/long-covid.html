In plain English

Research shows that many people experience persistent symptoms after covid infection (commonly called long COVID), most often fatigue and cognitive problems, and that rehabilitation — especially exercise-based programs and some online cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) or combined physical/mental-health programmes — can help some patients. Estimates of how common long COVID vary, and the biological mechanisms are still being worked out; there is not yet a single proven cure. PMID 35429399 PMID 39603702 PMID 37586104 PMID 39326415

Key findings

  • Pooled estimates from multiple studies suggest a substantial proportion of people report post-COVID-19 condition (long COVID); a meta-analysis estimated a global pooled prevalence around 43%, with fatigue (23%) and memory problems (14%) among the most common symptoms reported. Hospitalized patients had higher prevalence than non-hospitalized in that analysis. PMID 35429399
  • Fatigue and cognitive impairment are two of the most frequent and debilitating features of long COVID; a systematic review and meta-analysis focused on symptoms at ≥12 weeks found these to be common (the review pooled many studies to quantify proportions). PMID 34973396
  • Rehabilitation and exercise-based interventions show benefit in trials and pooled studies: a meta-analysis of exercise-based rehabilitation found improvements in dyspnea (breathlessness), fatigue, depression, walking distance (6-minute walk test), some lung-function measures, and quality of life after covid. PMID 37586104
  • A living systematic review of randomized trials (24 trials, 3,695 patients) found moderate-certainty evidence that an online CBT programme probably reduces fatigue and probably improves concentration compared with usual care, and that an online supervised combined physical + mental-health rehabilitation programme probably improves overall health and reduces depressive symptoms. The review covered drug and non-drug interventions across trials. PMID 39603702
  • Narrative and review articles summarize that long COVID can affect multiple organ systems, may cause severe functional impairment in some people, and that proposed mechanisms (including organ damage and immune/biological processes) are under active study; these reviews call for more mechanism-informed trials and investment in therapeutics. PMID 39096925 PMID 39326415

How strong is the evidence?

  • Evidence includes large meta-analyses and systematic reviews pooling dozens of observational studies for prevalence and symptom frequency, and randomized trials summarized in a living systematic review for some interventions. The living review included 24 randomized trials with 3,695 participants and reported moderate-certainty evidence for some non-drug interventions (online CBT, combined rehab) PMID 39603702; other intervention evidence comes from smaller trials pooled in meta-analyses (e.g., 23 studies, 1,579 participants for exercise programmes) PMID 37586104.
  • Mechanistic understanding is based on narrative reviews and emerging molecular studies; these syntheses highlight plausible biological pathways but do not yet provide definitive causal proof or a universal treatment. PMID 39326415 PMID 39096925

Limitations and caveats

  • Prevalence estimates vary by study design, population, follow-up timing, and region; meta-analyses pooled heterogeneous studies, so the exact prevalence number is uncertain. PMID 35429399
  • Many intervention studies differ in program content, duration, and patient selection; benefits seen for structured rehabilitation or online CBT may not generalize to all patients with long COVID. The living review reports "probably" for some effects, reflecting remaining uncertainty. PMID 39603702 PMID 37586104
  • Narrative reviews on mechanisms summarize hypotheses (organ damage, immune/inflammatory pathways, etc.) but do not prove a single cause; therapeutics based on these mechanisms remain experimental and under development. PMID 39326415 PMID 39096925
  • The articles include a mix of observational prevalence studies, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, and some randomized trials; where trial or pooled trial evidence is limited, conclusions about treatment effectiveness are preliminary. PMID 34973396 PMID 37586104
