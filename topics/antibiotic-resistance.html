In plain English

Antibiotic resistance is widespread, rising over time, and occurs across clinical pathogens and environmental bacteria. Resistance reduces the effectiveness of common treatments (for example, clarithromycin against Helicobacter pylori and many antibiotics against hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae), and environmental reservoirs (urban runoff, soil) and bacterial evolution contribute to its spread PMID 29990487 PMID 39069234 PMID 30826682.

Key findings

  • Global pooled data show clinically important resistance in Helicobacter pylori to clarithromycin, metronidazole and levofloxacin; clarithromycin resistance strongly predicts failure of clarithromycin-based treatment (odds ratio ~6.97) PMID 29990487.
  • A meta-analysis of hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (hvKp) strains found high resistance rates to many antibiotic classes, including substantial resistance to third- and fourth-generation cephalosporins and even carbapenems (e.g., imipenem 45.7%, meropenem 51.0% reported in the pooled studies) PMID 39069234.
  • Antibiotic use alters gut microbiota diversity and function, which can increase susceptibility to colonization and may promote resistance development; alternatives under study include microcins, prebiotics, probiotics, phage therapy and fecal microbial transplantation (reviewed) PMID 36537602.
  • Resistance genes often originate in environmental microorganisms and can move into pathogens; evolutionary factors (ecological connectivity, founder effects, fitness costs) modulate transfer of resistance from the environment to clinical pathogens (reviewed) PMID 24847651.
  • Aquatic environments exposed to urban runoff and wastewater are potential hotspots for selection and dissemination of antibiotic-resistant bacteria and resistance genes, implicating environmental reservoirs in the epidemiology of resistance (reviewed) PMID 30826682.
  • Reversing resistance in populations is difficult in practice because compensatory mutations and co-selection (linked resistance to other antibiotics) can stabilize resistance even when antibiotic pressure is reduced (reviewed) PMID 24836051.

How strong is the evidence?

The strongest direct evidence here comes from two systematic/meta-analytic studies: one pooling 178 studies and 66,142 H. pylori isolates, which links specific resistance to treatment failure PMID 29990487, and one meta-analysis of 77 studies reporting pooled resistance rates in hvKp PMID 39069234. The other included articles are narrative reviews that summarize mechanisms, environmental roles, and control concepts; these provide mechanistic and contextual support but are not pooled trial data PMID 36537602 PMID 24847651 PMID 30826682 PMID 24836051 PMID 9561819.

Limitations and caveats

  • The meta-analyses report substantial heterogeneity across studies and regions, so pooled percentages may mask large local differences (not all regions or settings behave the same) PMID 29990487 PMID 39069234.
  • Narrative reviews summarize mechanisms and possible interventions but do not provide randomized-trial evidence that alternative approaches (e.g., probiotics, phage therapy) reliably prevent or reverse resistance in humans PMID 36537602.
  • These abstracts do not provide guidance on clinical management or specific treatment choices; efficacy and safety of interventions require clinical-trial data, which are not contained in the provided abstracts PMID 29990487 PMID 39069234.
  • Environmental and evolutionary reviews describe plausible pathways for gene transfer and selection but cannot quantify the exact contribution of any single environmental reservoir to human infections from the abstracts alone PMID 24847651 PMID 30826682.
