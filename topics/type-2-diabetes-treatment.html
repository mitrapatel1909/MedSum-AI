In plain English

Research summarized in the provided abstracts shows several non-pharmacologic and adjunctive treatment approaches for type 2 diabetes (T2DM): structured exercise programs can improve some metabolic measures, ketogenic diets in overweight people with T2DM show benefits for weight, blood sugar (HbA1c), and some lipids, and certain supplements (cinnamon, curcumin) and microbiome-focused strategies are being studied as adjuncts. Reviews also emphasize the close links between obesity and T2DM and the rationale for multi-target drugs. Most evidence here comes from meta-analyses and narrative reviews rather than large definitive clinical outcome trials in every area.

Key findings

  • Structured exercise programs improved some measures related to insulin resistance and fasting glucose in T2DM in pooled trials; the meta-analysis of 11 trials (846 participants) found reductions in fasting blood sugar but mixed effects on fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, HbA1c and BMI PMID 30553010.
  • In overweight patients with T2DM, randomized controlled trials pooled in a meta-analysis (8 RCTs) reported that a ketogenic diet led to greater weight loss, reduced waist circumference, lower HbA1c, lower triglycerides, and higher HDL cholesterol compared with control diets PMID 36012064.
  • Cinnamon supplementation was associated with statistically significant reductions in fasting blood glucose, HOMA-IR (a calculated index of insulin resistance), and HbA1c across randomized controlled trials in an updated dose–response meta-analysis of 24 RCTs, though insulin levels did not change significantly PMID 37818728.
  • Curcumin (a bioactive from turmeric) has preclinical evidence (animal and cell studies) suggesting potential benefits on beta-cell function, insulin resistance, and prevention of diabetes development; clinical trials and mechanisms are reviewed but evidence comes from a mix of preclinical and clinical studies summarized in a narrative review PMID 31398884.
  • The gut microbiota is implicated in T2DM pathogenesis and is a proposed target for interventions (drugs, probiotics, prebiotics, fecal microbiota transplantation, diet) to modify metabolic outcomes, based on mechanistic and review literature PMID 35242721.
  • Obesity and T2DM share pathophysiology; many treatments affect both conditions (some anti-diabetic drugs affect weight), and bariatric surgery is noted as highly effective for both obesity and T2DM in reviews of epidemiology and treatments PMID 37152942.
  • Multi-target drug strategies (e.g., incretin agonists, SGLT inhibitors, PPAR modulators) and phytocomplexes are discussed as approaches to address the multiple pathways involved in T2DM in narrative review literature PMID 32340373.
  • Observational studies included in a systematic review indicate that overall self-care (diet, exercise, glucose monitoring) is often suboptimal in adults with T2DM, with exercise being one of the least adhered-to behaviors PMID 31267873.

How strong is the evidence?

  • The strongest-level evidence in these abstracts comes from meta-analyses of randomized trials (exercise meta-analysis, ketogenic diet meta-analysis, cinnamon supplementation meta-analysis) which pool multiple studies [PMID 30553010; PMID 36012064; PMID 37818728]. The exercise meta-analysis included 11 trials and 846 participants PMID 30553010; the ketogenic diet meta-analysis pooled 8 RCTs PMID 36012064; the cinnamon meta-analysis pooled 24 RCTs PMID 37818728.
  • Other conclusions are based on narrative reviews and mechanistic/preclinical studies, which summarize possible mechanisms and early clinical findings but do not provide pooled trial-level proof [PMID 31398884; PMID 32340373; PMID 35242721; PMID 37152942].
  • Consistency varies: some pooled analyses found clear benefits (e.g., ketogenic diet on weight and HbA1c; cinnamon on several glycemic markers), while the exercise meta-analysis showed mixed results across specific endpoints [PMID 36012064; PMID 37818728; PMID 30553010].

Limitations and caveats

  • These abstracts do not provide long-term clinical outcome data (e.g., complications, mortality) and focus mainly on intermediate outcomes (weight, glucose, lipids, insulin resistance) [PMID 30553010; PMID 36012064; PMID 37818728].
  • Heterogeneity across trials (differences in interventions, durations, participant characteristics) can affect meta-analysis results; full trial details and heterogeneity measures are not provided in the abstracts [PMID 30553010; PMID 36012064; PMID 37818728].
  • Some evidence is preclinical or from narrative reviews (curcumin, microbiota mechanisms, multi-target drug rationale), so clinical benefits in humans remain preliminary or not fully established in these abstracts [PMID 31398884; PMID 35242721; PMID 32340373].
  • The systematic review on self-care reports poor adherence but cannot by itself show which interventions reliably improve adherence or patient-important outcomes PMID 31267873.
