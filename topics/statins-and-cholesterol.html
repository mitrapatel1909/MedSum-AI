In plain English

Randomized trials show that lowering LDL cholesterol (LDL-C) reduces major cardiovascular events. More intensive LDL-C lowering (target <55 mg/dL) reduced events versus a conventional target (<70 mg/dL) in a recent randomized trial, and large trials of nonstatin LDL-C–lowering drugs that further reduce LDL-C on top of statins also lowered cardiovascular events. Other trials show substantial LDL-C lowering with new agents (PCSK9 inhibitors, inclisiran) though not all had clinical-outcome data in these abstracts. PMID 41910315 PMID 28304224 PMID 36876740 PMID 32187462

Key findings

  • A randomized trial of patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease found that targeting LDL-C <55 mg/dL vs <70 mg/dL (median achieved 56 vs 66 mg/dL) reduced the 3-year composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke, revascularization, or hospitalization for unstable angina (hazard ratio 0.67). Safety end points were similar between groups in that trial. PMID 41910315
  • In a large randomized, placebo-controlled trial (27,564 patients on statins), the PCSK9 inhibitor evolocumab lowered LDL-C by ~59% (median baseline 92 mg/dL to 30 mg/dL) and significantly reduced the composite of cardiovascular death, MI, stroke, unstable-angina hospitalization, or revascularization (HR 0.85) and the key composite of cardiovascular death/MI/stroke (HR 0.80). Results were consistent across subgroups. PMID 28304224
  • In statin-intolerant patients, the ATP-citrate lyase inhibitor bempedoic acid reduced LDL-C (mean baseline ~139 mg/dL; absolute reduction ~29.2 mg/dL at 6 months) and reduced the primary composite cardiovascular outcome over median 40.6 months (HR 0.87). PMID 36876740
  • Inclisiran, an siRNA that lowers hepatic PCSK9 production, produced sustained ~50% reductions in LDL-C with dosing every 6 months in two phase 3 trials; those abstracts report lipid reductions and safety but not clinical-outcome results. PMID 32187462
  • Reviews summarize that statins are the mainstay for lowering LDL-C and preventing cardiovascular disease, discuss statin adverse effects (especially muscle symptoms), and describe newer nonstatin LDL-C–lowering therapies (PCSK9 inhibitors, inclisiran, bempedoic acid) as alternatives or additions. These reviews discuss mechanisms and clinical considerations but are narrative (not pooled outcome trials). PMID 37956957 PMID 30653440 PMID 28057795 PMID 37567512

How strong is the evidence?

The strongest evidence in these abstracts comes from randomized controlled trials, including one large outcomes trial of a PCSK9 inhibitor (n=27,564) and other randomized outcome trials (the intensive-target LDL trial with 3,048 patients and the bempedoic acid trial with ~13,970 patients). These are randomized clinical trials measuring hard cardiovascular endpoints, which provides high-quality evidence that additional LDL-C lowering reduces events in secondary-prevention populations. Some abstracts (inclisiran trials) report large, well-conducted phase 3 randomized trials for LDL-C lowering but did not report clinical-outcome results in the provided abstracts.

Limitations and caveats

  • These abstracts primarily address patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or high risk; they do not directly report effects in all primary-prevention groups.
  • Not every LDL-C–lowering agent in these abstracts reported clinical-outcome results here (inclisiran abstracts report lipid changes; outcome effects are not included in those abstracts). PMID 32187462
  • Narrative reviews describe mechanisms, pleiotropic effects, and adverse effects but do not provide pooled or randomized-outcome evidence; they summarize background and hypotheses rather than proving causal effects. PMID 28057795 PMID 30653440 PMID 37567512
  • These abstracts do not provide individual-patient guidance, long-term noncardiovascular safety beyond trial duration, or comparisons of all possible LDL-C targets across diverse populations.
